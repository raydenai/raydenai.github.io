# Expanded AURA Visual Directions: Two Further Brand Worlds

The first review set already establishes three useful modes: **Authority Film** for high-stakes presence, **Proof Machine** for outcome-led credibility, and **Idea Studio** for intellectual property. This second set deliberately moves farther away from the safe “consultant website” middle. Each route has a different emotional temperature, conversion mechanism, and photography requirement.

> **Prototype rule:** These are not theme swaps. A useful direction changes what leads the page, what the visitor believes first, how photography behaves, and which conversion action feels natural.

## 05 — The Private Office

**Core statement:** “The work is private. The standard should be visible.”

The Private Office route is a high-discretion editorial-luxury system for board advisors, family-office strategists, elite executive coaches, confidential consultants, and specialist firms. It does not signal scale through loud motion or statistics. It signals it through **restraint, rarity, and considered detail**: deep parchment, ink, oxblood, an archival serif, blind-deboss style rules, large margins, a visual seal, and an invitation-oriented information hierarchy.

| Design element | Direction | Why it feels powerful |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Quiet editorial portrait or interior, compact statement, oversized serif, a formal “private advisory” marker | Creates scarcity and maturity instead of generic premium styling |
| Signature device | Numbered advisory dispatches and a seal / monogram | Makes the site feel like a private institution, not a freelancer landing page |
| Proof | Discreet “trusted by” references, anonymized pattern statements, closed-room context | Fits confidential work without forcing logos or invented metrics |
| Offer | Three invitation cards: retain, convene, intervene | Reframes services as levels of access and responsibility |
| Conversion | “Request a private conversation” with qualification note | Protects positioning and attracts a high-intent lead |

### Photography directive

Use controlled, tactile images rather than generic luxury cues. Shoot a calm environmental portrait in a library, architectural office, gallery, hotel lounge, or desk setting with window light. Capture the hand on a marked page, a notebook, an unopened envelope, and a cropped room detail. Avoid luxury stock imagery, champagne, rented private jets, or false exclusivity.

### Best fit and risk

This route is strongest when the buyer cares about judgement, discretion, and proximity to consequential decisions. It fails when used to hide a lack of proof or when the brand needs high volume, energetic media, or a broad consumer audience.

## 06 — The Category Signal

**Core statement:** “The category is wrong. Here is the better operating system.”

The Category Signal route is a decisive, contrarian manifesto system for founders, transformation leaders, technologists, growth experts, and category creators. It treats the homepage as a visual argument: a sharp thesis, edited “old way / new way” contrasts, a visible named framework, annotated evidence, and a red/orange or acid-lime signal color over near-black. It is designed to create the feeling that the visitor has encountered a different model, not a better-looking consultant.

| Design element | Direction | Why it feels powerful |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Poster-scale, punchy thesis with a deliberately constrained portrait or product artifact | Establishes a memorable position before biography |
| Signature device | Struck-through assumptions and “replace this with that” decision rails | Converts contrarian thinking into a repeatable visual language |
| Proof | Dated opinion excerpts, annotated cases, before / after operating maps | Lets evidence support a bold claim without making it vague |
| Method | One large diagnostic map with a named sequence | Makes the intellectual property feel like a real asset |
| Conversion | “Run the diagnostic” or “See if the old model is costing you” | Gives the buyer a self-directed next move |

### Photography directive

Shoot hard, direct, and active. Use an environmental working image, a close speaking frame, a moving walk-through, whiteboard detail, or a decisive hand gesture. Lighting may be high contrast and editorial; wardrobe may include one strong color block. The subject should look like a person explaining or changing a system, not posing for a corporate annual report.

### Best fit and risk

This route works when the person has a genuinely differentiated methodology and can substantiate a contrarian thesis. It fails if the claim is generic, adversarial for its own sake, or unsupported by case evidence. The manifesto must generate clarity, not noise.

## The expanded selection map

| Route | Buyer emotion first | What leads | Visual temperature | Photography most needed | Conversion posture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Authority Film | “This person has been in consequential rooms.” | Context and crucible | Cinematic, controlled | Stage, boardroom, credible environment | Strategic conversation |
| Proof Machine | “There is a real record here.” | Results and receipts | Analytical, sharp | Documentary work and artifacts | Evidence diagnostic |
| Idea Studio | “This person can make my thinking clearer.” | Point of view | Editorial, warm | Studio, desk, objects, seated portrait | Useful artifact |
| Private Office | “This is judgment I want close to the decision.” | Discretion and standard | Quiet, archival, elevated | Interior, tactility, quiet portrait | Private conversation |
| Category Signal | “This is a model I have not seen before.” | Contrarian thesis | Bold, graphic, direct | Active work, speaking, whiteboard | Run the diagnostic |

The planned prototypes should use only conceptual copy and clearly marked fictional data. A production site should use the route whose actual proof, photography, buyer, and conversion goal most closely match the table.
