# AURA Personal-Brand Photography Shoot Guide and Pose Blueprint

**Purpose:** This document turns the strongest recurring patterns in the InfluEx authority-site corpus into a usable photographer brief, on-set plan, pose system, styling guide, and digital-delivery specification.

**Research basis:** Twenty-seven reachable InfluEx-led personal-brand sites with real photography were audited. The strongest repeated hero formula is a waist-up, side-third, direct-gaze, tailored portrait lit by a large soft key and rim light. Cut-out heroes appeared on 17 of 30 corpus sites, while real environmental and stage imagery carried the most credibility on the highest-scoring authority brands. [1] [2]

> **The goal is not to look “professional.” The goal is to create a visual record that proves the person belongs in the rooms their buyer cares about.**

## 1. Choose the visual treatment before booking the shoot

A visual treatment is a business choice. Select it by the buyer’s context and available evidence, not by the photographer’s default portfolio.

| Treatment | Visual promise | Best client categories | Hero image | Essential evidence image | Reference logic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Obsidian Authority** | Controlled, high-status, decisive | Executive advisors, investors, legal, enterprise consulting | Transparent tailored portrait on deep surface | Boardroom / working image | Dark authority brands use restraint and separation, not excess decoration. [1] |
| **Editorial Ivory** | Clear, human, high-trust, intellectual | Authors, leadership coaches, premium strategists | High-key seamless portrait with genuine soft shadow | Studio narrative / marked-up work | Kathryn Janicek’s authority is carried by proof, real story, and clear hierarchy instead of a dark “premium” surface. [3] |
| **Executive In-Situ** | This person visibly does the work | Operators, speakers, firm principals, pragmatic consultants | Environmental hero with intentional text negative space | Workshop, boardroom, site visit, or stage | Vinh Giang’s ecosystem uses real action and the world around the person as proof. [4] |
| **Idea Studio** | Original thinking made tangible | Creators, product strategists, authors, coaches | Editorial thesis with portrait as secondary reveal | Desk, objects, annotated method artifacts | Pam Hendrickson demonstrates warm audience language and method-led clarity. [5] |

The first demo used Obsidian Authority. For the next prototype, do **not** repeat the same cut-out hero by default. If the client has a real setting or real stage history, Executive In-Situ or Authority Film is more credible and more distinctive.

## 2. The non-negotiable eight-shot library

A usable personal-brand website requires a narrative, not 40 versions of the same headshot. Produce all eight roles in one planned session, with stage images captured at a genuine event when speaking is part of the offer.

| ID | Image role | Where it appears | Minimum frame count | Desktop crop | Mobile crop | What it must communicate |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| `01` | Hero cut-out | Split hero, nav overlay, page opener | 10–12 | Tall transparent PNG; subject in outer third | Centered 4:5 transparent crop | Authority and directness |
| `02` | Hero in-situ | Alternative hero, banner, speaking page | 6–8 | 16:9 with 45–55% copy-side negative space | 4:5 centered face / torso | Context and real world |
| `03` | Story environmental | About, origin, long-form essay | 8–10 | 3:2 or 16:9 | 4:5 | Thoughtfulness and the origin of the work |
| `04` | Stage wide | Speaker page, proof, final CTA | 5–8 | 16:9 wide, visible room scale | 4:5 crop preserving face and audience | Public authority and scale |
| `05` | Stage tight | Speaker proof, video, testimonial band | 5–8 | 3:2 | 4:5 | Energy, command, and expression |
| `06` | Working candid | Offer, process, case-study pages | 8–12 | 3:2 | 4:5 | Real advisory / teaching work in progress |
| `07` | Seated editorial | Contact, manifesto, footer, press bio | 8–10 | 4:5 / 3:2 | 4:5 | Approachability after competence has been established |
| `08` | Detail / texture | CTA bands, story sections, social cards | 10–15 | 16:9 | 4:5 | Tactility, signature objects, and visual breath |

The audit’s median site used roughly ten homepage images, but those images were effective only when they played distinct roles. [1] The library above produces enough variety for a homepage, six supporting pages, articles, social crops, and future changes without repeated imagery.

## 3. Studio hero session: exact pose blueprint

The studio hero session is a 45–60 minute block. It begins with safe, reusable poses and moves toward more specific expressions. Use an 85mm lens for primary waist-up frames, an 50mm lens for seated and environmental context, and capture vertical, horizontal, and negative-space variants before changing lighting.

### Pose family A — Core authority portraits

| Pose | Body direction | Head / gaze | Hands | On-set instruction | Best use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1. Anchored direct** | Feet shoulder-width, torso 20° away from camera, weight on back leg | Eyes to lens, chin 1 cm forward then slightly down | Relaxed at sides or one thumb in trouser pocket | “You have just walked into the room to make the decision.” | Hero cut-out | Square shoulders; stiff military stance |
| **A2. Open three-quarter** | Torso 45° away; front shoulder closer to camera | Eyes to lens, slight eyebrow lift | One hand resting near jacket button | “Turn your chest away; keep your attention with me.” | Hero / about | Over-twisting neck toward camera |
| **A3. Measured crossed arms** | Three-quarter body, elbows low and relaxed | Direct or just past lens | Hands tucked, no clenched fists | “This is certainty, not defensiveness—leave space between arms and torso.” | Finance, legal, executive advisory | High crossed arms; hands hidden; compressed shoulders |
| **A4. Jacket adjustment** | Three-quarter body with forward micro-step | Gaze just off lens | One hand adjusts cuff or lapel | “Reset your jacket once, then pause as if you are about to begin.” | Motion-like hero still / editorial | Frozen mid-pull, visible tension |
| **A5. Conversation stance** | Torso toward imagined client just off camera | Gaze 15° off lens; mouth neutral or restrained half-smile | One hand gestures at mid-torso, other relaxed | “Explain the one thing your clients finally understand after working with you.” | Method / offer section | Open-palmed sales gesture held too long |

### Pose family B — Intellect and narrative

| Pose | Body direction | Head / gaze | Hands / prop | On-set instruction | Best use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B1. Seated forward** | Sit on chair edge, ankles grounded, torso slightly forward | Eyes to lens or 10° off | One forearm rests lightly on thigh | “Listen as though the client has just said the real problem.” | Contact, manifesto | Slouching, knees toward camera, crossed ankles |
| **B2. Seated profile thought** | Chair 60–90° side-on near window / desk | Look out of frame, chin neutral | One hand touches notebook / desk, other at rest | “Think about the turning point that changed your work.” | Origin story | Hand to chin or generic ‘thinking’ pose |
| **B3. The annotated method** | Standing / seated at wall, glass, or large paper | Eyes on artifact—not camera | Marker, pencil, or notebook visible | “Mark the exact moment the framework becomes useful.” | Method block / articles | Fake scribbles, unreadable confidential content |
| **B4. The artifact hold** | Centered or three-quarter, chest-to-waist frame | Gentle direct gaze | Hold actual book, report, framework card, or tool naturally | “Show the object you would hand a serious buyer.” | Book, lead magnet, named method | Fake book cover, product obscuring the face |

### Pose family C — Movement and energy

| Pose | Body direction | Head / gaze | Hands | On-set instruction | Best use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C1. Directional walk** | Walk across a controlled path parallel to camera | Look toward destination, not lens | Natural stride, one hand adjusts jacket | “Walk as if you are late for the room that needs you.” | Hero in-situ, transitions | Mid-stride blur without a purposeful shutter choice |
| **C2. Threshold arrival** | Stop in doorway, hall, or architectural frame | Gaze in lens after turning from space | One hand at side, one slightly forward | “You have just entered before anyone has spoken.” | Authority Film hero | Cliché doorway silhouette that hides the face |
| **C3. Work explanation** | Standing by actual project, whiteboard, or screen | Look to client / collaborator, not camera | Natural one-hand gesture | “Teach this to a person, not to the photographer.” | Working candid | Photographer-directed fake meeting pose |
| **C4. Stage command** | Real stage only; grounded feet, room-aware posture | Varies between audience scan and camera | Gesture created by actual sentence / movement | “Deliver the line that always makes the room go quiet.” | Speaking, video, proof | Empty-stage simulation presented as event proof |

### Pose family D — Human warmth after proof

| Pose | Body direction | Head / gaze | Hands | On-set instruction | Best use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1. Quiet warmth** | Seated 30° angle, shoulders lowered | Soft direct gaze; genuine micro-smile | One hand lightly touching armrest / knee | “Think of a client who finally got the result.” | Contact / footer | Broad corporate smile |
| **D2. Small laugh release** | Seated / standing, slightly turned | Eyes off lens, mid-laugh only after a real prompt | Unscripted | Ask a collaborator for a specific funny memory; capture the release | About / social | Laughing at camera, over-produced joy |
| **D3. Hands at work** | No face necessary | N/A | Writing, turning a page, preparing a mic, opening a book | “Do the work slowly; do not perform it.” | Texture / article / CTA | Generic laptop typing stock image |

## 4. The four hero compositions

The hero needs its own art direction. Do not decide hero cropping during web development. Capture it during the shoot.

| Composition | Camera / crop | Placement | Text safe zone | Best treatment | Technical delivery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cut-out split** | 85mm, waist-up, tall frame | Subject in far left or right third | Opposite 50% uninterrupted | Obsidian Authority | Alpha PNG: desktop 1200px high and mobile 800px high |
| **Cinematic environmental** | 35–50mm, 16:9 | Subject in outer third or small within architectural field | Opposite half with lower detail / exposure | Authority Film / Executive In-Situ | Desktop 2400×1350; separate 4:5 mobile crop |
| **Editorial centered thesis** | 50–85mm, 4:5 or square | Centered, calm, surrounded by whitespace | Text above / below rather than over image | Idea Studio / Editorial Ivory | 1600×2000 vertical and 1080×1350 social crop |
| **Stage scale** | 24–35mm, 16:9 wide | Person surrounded by real audience / stage context | Top or side dark field if available | Performance World | Full-resolution RAW plus a 2400px web master |

## 5. Lighting diagrams in words

### A. Obsidian Authority / cut-out

Place a 4–5 foot octabox 45° from camera and approximately 20° above eye line. Place a narrow stripbox behind and opposite the key, angled to catch hair and outer shoulder. Keep fill at approximately 1:4 for calm authority; reduce toward 1:8 for drama. Shoot the cut-out against neutral grey, not green, to reduce hair contamination. The background is an intentional digital surface, not something to light on set.

### B. Editorial Ivory / high key

Use a large octabox just above and 30–45° from camera. Add broad reflector or soft fill at 1:2. Light a white background independently 1–2 stops brighter than the subject so it reaches clean white without stealing the shoulder edge. Retain a soft physical ground shadow: it stops the person appearing artificially pasted into the page.

### C. Executive In-Situ / real environment

Start with the environment’s actual light. Use window light or a large soft source from one side as key, use the room as fill, and add a low-level rim only when hair or tailored dark clothing disappears into the space. Use f/2–f/2.8 so the setting reads as a credible place while remaining subordinate to the face.

### D. Stage / event

Never force a controlled studio light onto an actual event. Expose for the face, preserve the stage color / audience context, shoot silently where required, and capture both the person’s best expression and a wide frame that proves the room. An honest smaller room is more credible than a fabricated large one.

## 6. Wardrobe and grooming plan

The corpus strongly converges on tailored, neutral wardrobe: black/charcoal/navy plus white/cream, with **one saturated element maximum**. Tailored presentation consistently outperformed casual hero imagery in the audit. [1]

| Look | Purpose | Clothing formula | Color rule | Shoes / accessories |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Look 1 — Authority** | Hero cut-out, stage, premium offer | Structured blazer or suit; crisp shirt / blouse / fine knit | Dark base + light inner layer | Polished minimal shoe; one watch / small jewelry; no visible brand marks |
| **Look 2 — Human** | Story, seated, contact | Softer tailoring, knit, open collar, texture | Slightly lighter / warmer version of Look 1 | Remove anything noisy or overly formal |
| **Look 3 — Work** | In-situ, workshop, candid | Same palette, practical layers | Must read as same person and same brand | Real work objects only |
| **Optional accent look** | Editorial Ivory or Performance World | One deliberate saturated jacket, dress, tie, knit, or footwear accent | Match brand accent; every other visible garment neutral | Use once; do not repeat as a costume |

Hair, makeup, and grooming should aim for camera-ready realism, not a different person. Matte skin is important under key lights. Bring lint roller, garment steamer, translucent powder, spare shirt / blouse, comfortable shoes for walking shots, and a non-reflective water bottle.

## 8. Photographer and retoucher deliverables

| Deliverable | Specification | Website use |
|---|---|---|
| RAW archive | All selected finals plus essential alternates | Future recrops and brand evolution |
| Master retouches | 40–55 final full-resolution files | Source of truth for web / print |
| Hero alpha cut-outs | High-resolution PNG with clean natural hair edge | Split hero, profile card, motion overlays |
| Desktop derivatives | WebP / JPEG at 2400px max long edge | High-density desktop layout |
| Mobile art-directed crops | Separate 4:5 or square per hero / story frame | Small screens without face loss |
| Social crops | 1080×1350, 1080×1080, 1920×1080 | LinkedIn, Instagram, video thumbnails |
| Grade reference | One approved reference image plus LUT / preset note | Consistency across future sessions |
| Asset manifest | Filename, role, alt suggestion, release / consent status | Content authoring and QA |

Run the supplied `pnpm photos:build` command after masters are placed in the private master directory. Run `pnpm verify:images` before build to catch missing assets and incorrect dimensions.

## 9. Authenticity and legal guardrails

Do not fabricate a speaking event, celebrity association, client meeting, published book, or business result in photography. Concept work can be beautiful; it must be labeled and must not function as fake proof. When a real client or collaborator appears, obtain a written usage release. When an office, screen, whiteboard, or property appears, remove confidential or personally identifiable information before publication.

## 10. Pre-shoot checklist

| Owner | Before shoot | Confirmed when |
|---|---|---|
| Strategist | Select visual treatment and page map | Every shot has a page / persuasion role |
| Client | Approve wardrobe looks and real locations | Location communicates the intended buyer context |
| Photographer | Build lighting diagram and shot board | Both desktop and mobile crops are planned |
| Producer | Confirm releases, props, travel, stage access | Legal / access risk removed |
| Retoucher | Approve grade reference and cut-out standard | No halo, skin texture preserved, one grade across set |
| Developer | Create asset manifest and content placeholders | Images map to real AURA block fields |
